The Lore

Everything the story stands on — the dying Earth, the timeline of the end, the powers of the last years, the people aboard the Tamaraw, and the maps of the world being left behind.

The Dying Earth

The World Had Three Years Left
When It Finally United

Tamaraw Exodus is set at the end of Earth — not in fire, but in exhaustion. The nations spent themselves in a war with no end, and when it was over, the world was already dying. Too late, mankind united as one nation. Too late to save the Earth — but just in time to leave it.

The Long War

Years of tension broke down into a war that seemed to have no end — ambitions, grudges, and rivalry winning every time diplomacy lost. And when the fighting finally stopped, it stopped because there was nothing left to fight over.

The Devastation

The plants that fed us rotted and withered. The animals that sustained us tainted and decayed. The land softened and crumbled, the water dried up, and the atmosphere that protected us weakened into disrepair. The world did not fall in a battle. It was simply, patiently spent.

The Three Years

A man desiring peace subjugated the clans and returned unity to the world — and with it, the truth: Earth had three years left before it became mankind's grave. Mankind finally achieved the unity it could not find in plenty. It was, and is, too late — except for one miracle.

The Story So Far

The World, in Five Movements

Every event is a cause and a reaction — nothing lands on the same day it begins. Read the eras below as you would read the sea: the storm was announced long before it arrived.

Era I

The Long War

Years −30 to −1

The nations of the world break down into a war with no end. Ambition, grudges, and rivalry win every diplomacy. The Philippines, like the rest, spends its fleets and its islands in the fighting.

Cause: decades of tension, spent resources, no will to stop.

Reaction: a world exhausted — the war ends only when there is nothing left to fight over.

Era I → II

The Devastation

Years −1 to 0

The war ends — and with it, the world. The plants rot, the animals decay, the land crumbles, the water dries, the air thins. Remnants fight over the last supplies, and a second war breaks out, driven by fear and greed.

Cause: a dead world, and desperate people.

Reaction: a man desiring peace subjugates the clans and returns unity to the world.

Era II

The Unification

Year 0

One man ends the war, and all mankind becomes one nation, working as one toward the same goal — the unity it could never find in plenty. And then the truth comes out: Earth has three more years before it becomes mankind's grave.

Cause: unity, won too late.

Reaction: mankind stops fighting to survive, and starts working to be remembered.

Era II → III

The Three Years

Years 0 to 3

As mankind gives up on saving the world and focuses on preserving its legacy, a miracle happens: a continent similar to the mythical Atlantis rises from the sea — and with it, a gate leading to the unknown. Salvation, or doom. No one knows yet.

Cause: the gate, and the last of Earth's resources.

Reaction: the evacuation begins. Every nation builds what it can carry.

Era III

The Exodus — Chapter I

Present · the port of Manila

In the ruins of the prosperous port city Manila, the retrofit of the Tamaraw has begun — the capital ship of what was once the Philippines, salvaged from the wrecks of the wars of old. A frigate-sized command vessel, built to lead the evacuation fleet through the gate, and to carry the last of a nation.

Cause: the emergency government's orders — and one man's refusal to leave anyone behind.

Reaction: Chapter I, "Feats of Unity." Read it on the Story page.

Nations & Factions

The Powers of the Last Years

The factions are drawn from the story as written — and each is a size of the same tale, from one person to all of mankind. A territory map of the Philippine isles will sit here when the plate is restored.

The One Nation

All of mankind, under one banner, united in this era of scarce — the unity that was impossible in plenty, achieved when there was nothing left to share. One goal, now: to leave, and to be remembered.

Scale: from a single citizen to all of Earth.

The Emergency Government

The hand that builds the fleet. Its orders reach Manila from the seat of the new world order: finish the Tamaraw, and remember the directive — that the last of the settlers is second only to the survival of the ship.

Scale: from one order to the fate of a nation.

The Stayers

"Those nutjobs back home" — the ones who chose faith over the fleet. Temples and bunkers, waiting for rapture instead of a gate. To the crew of the Tamaraw, they are the ones who gave up. To themselves, they are the only ones who stayed true. The Kaysakat Holy Fire Church is one of these old faiths — its place in the story is yours to write.

Scale: from one shrine to a world that chose to stay.

The Evacuation Fleet

The Tamaraw, the militia ships that will follow her, and the barges she must carry through troubled and calm waters alike. Not a navy — a nation in transit, made of yesterday's civilians, enlistment credit, and the desperate arithmetic of who fits aboard.

Scale: from one crewmate to the last of a people.

Mythos

What the Old World Believed
— and What Appeared

The story is told by people who grew up on the myths of the old world — and who are now watching one of them come true.

Atlantis

The drowned continent of myth — a place that should only exist in stories. And then, with three years left, a continent similar to the mythical Atlantis appears. The old stories never said what to do when the myth rises to meet you.

The Gate

A gate leading to the unknown, set in a land that should not exist. Salvation, or doom — the story has not decided, and neither has anyone aboard the fleet. Every ship that passes through it is a vote.

The Old Faiths

The religionists who chose temples and bunkers over ships — waiting for rapture, certain they will live forever. To the fleet they are the ones who gave up. But they are also the ones who never stopped believing the world could be saved.
